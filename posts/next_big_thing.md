I have everything a 24-year-old person of my background can possibly want. A good job, a loving relationship, no debt. Nothing extraordinary, but enough. I can eat what I want, buy most things I want, rent a place all by myself, take a sip on my 5 dollar cup of matcha latte in a cafe and write blogs (as I am doing right now, how meta!). But looking back on life, I have come a long way. Growing up I could barely have anything I wanted. I remember I had to study well, score top 3 in my class and nag my mother endlessly so she would buy me a tabletop war game (I don't know the name) that cost less than 5 dollars! Own place? I didn't even have my own room for majority of my life.

Once you have all these luxuries, it becomes harder and harder to appreciate the 5 dollars. You ignore everything you have ever known and grew up in, the moment you get out of it. You become unappreciative of what you have. I can't be content with having all I ever wanted, because the moment I have it, there's more to have. More everything. I also have forgotten what I wanted to do once I had things I really wanted. Did I want to help people? Help parents? Settle down? What did I really want when I was younger? I don't know or remember it anymore, because in the process of achieving my goals, I have forgotten why I set those goals. Setting goals and achieving them has become the point, at the expense of everything else. I've become more and more complacent with perpetually striving for the next goal, without knowing when to end this pursuit, sit down and focus on what's in front of me and around. I'm complacent about not having my own opinion, or own objectives. I just follow the next move that a person in my stage should follow. I don't stop and think, is that really something I need? Where does it end? I am complacent about not ever knowing that, but I'm starting to think, maybe I should not be.
