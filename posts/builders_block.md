# the art of shortest path
I am naturally a lazy person, which always made me search for the most minimal solution for the problems I am solving. When I solved an engineering problem, my goal was
always elegance and almost never "just get it done". This is a good skill to have, because good software is supposed to be as simple as possible, as complex as necessary 
(sorry Jensen Huang for stealing your line), because a simpler program is easier to extend, easier to explain and easier to write.

This has become a double edged sword, the intuition that helped me develop the skill of pinpointing the most minimal surface to solve a problem is the same intuition that is slowly
driving me to become lazier and lazier. I was always good at filling up blank pages, but I always took shortest path to get there, but I think this needs to stop now. 

# the art of filling a blank page
My ability to fill a blank page is slowly withering away, because everytime I look at one, my brain goes - "ugh, please ask an agent to fill most of it, you can just review",
and honestly I take that advice from my brain 9/10 times. But I realize, it was not always this way. There was a time, when I used to do majority of the early draft, the
thinking, the interface design, the contract, and let the agents do the refinement. But slowly, I seem to have taken a back seat, and its taking the life out of the builder in me.

I am suffering from what I shall call a "builder's block", I am sure I have not lost the ability to read or write good programs and build software, but I have somewhat lost the ability to
do it from scratch, and I attribute it completely to discipline.

# what now?
This stops, today. I have decided to ritualize my workflow, and have strict discipline on when to and when not to use AI, and which parts I really need to hand of to AI for productivity,
and not blindly rely on it to do majority of the work. Hopefully I will write an update on it soon.
